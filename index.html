Haveo — Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 19, 2026

TL;DR: Almost everything you write in Haveo stays on your phone. There is no account and no database of yours on our side. When you ask Haveo for help, what you typed goes to two companies — Anthropic, which writes the reply, and OpenAI, which speaks it aloud — and Haveo asks your permission before that happens for the first time. Haveo also counts which features get opened, so we can see which parts of the app help; a count is the name of a feature and nothing else, and it can't carry anything you wrote. Nothing is sold, and nothing tracks you across other apps.

What Haveo keeps on your phone

  • Your name, role, industry, bio and interests
  • The events you add, including anything you paste into the notes for one
  • Your intros, pitches, talking points and cheat sheet
  • Your reflections, wins and quick notes
  • The people you record having met, and what you wrote about them
  • Your conversations with Haveo
  • Your settings

This is stored by the app on your own device. It is not uploaded, it is not backed up to us, and it is not part of an account anywhere. If you delete the app, it is gone — including from us, because we never had it.

The microphone

Haveo can listen when you choose to speak instead of type — dictating a message, or practising out loud in Role-Play. Nothing is recorded or kept: your speech is turned into text, the text is what the app uses, and the audio is discarded. iOS asks your permission the first time, and you can withdraw it at any time in iOS Settings.

Where your device can transcribe speech on the phone itself, Haveo asks it to, and the audio never leaves your device. On devices or languages where that is not available, iOS transcribes it using Apple's speech recognition service, which means Apple handles the audio under Apple's privacy policy. Haveo never receives or keeps the audio either way.

Notifications

Haveo can remind you about an event you added. If you allow it, those reminders are scheduled by your phone, on your phone. There is no server sending you messages, and we cannot send you anything — Haveo has no push notifications at all. You can turn reminders off in iOS Settings.

What goes to Anthropic

Haveo's replies are written by Claude, made by Anthropic. When you use an AI feature, your request travels over HTTPS through a relay we run, which forwards it to Anthropic and returns the answer. The relay does not keep your conversations.

What we send depends on what you asked for:

  • Any AI feature: your name, role, industry and interests, and the details of the event you are prepping for — so the reply fits your situation.
  • Chat, Role-Play, intros, pitches, talking points: the messages you write, and anything you pasted into that event's notes.
  • Attaching a file: the contents of the PDF or text file you chose, so it can be read.
  • Asking what to try next time, after an event: what you wrote in your reflection — your wins, the moment that mattered, and where it got hard.
  • Asking Haveo to draft a message to someone you met: that person's name, where you met them, and the note you wrote about them.
  • Practising out loud: the questions and answers you rehearse, plus the intro, pitch and talking points you saved for that event, so the practice partner asks about your real situation rather than a made-up one.
  • Opening an event page: that event's name, type, category and your notes about it, so Haveo can write the short “what to expect” list. This is the one thing sent without you tapping anything, it happens once per event, and it does not happen at all if you have not turned the AI on.

Everything above is sent for one event at a time. Your other events, the reflections you have not asked for coaching on, and the notes about people you have not asked for a draft about, stay on your phone.

What goes to OpenAI

Haveo's spoken voice is made by OpenAI. When it reads a reply aloud, the text of that sentence goes through the same relay to OpenAI to be turned into audio. Only that sentence is sent — not your profile, not your event, not the rest of the conversation.

If you turn AI features off, your phone's own built-in voice is used instead, and nothing is sent.

How long they keep it

Neither company uses your conversations to train their AI models. Both delete what they receive within about 30 days, which they keep only to catch abuse of their systems. After that it is gone from them too. Their own policies say this in full: Anthropic and OpenAI.

Notes about other people

Haveo lets you write down who you met and what you want to remember about them. Those notes live on your phone, and we never see them.

There is one exception, and it is worth knowing before you use it: if you ask Haveo to draft a follow-up message to someone, that person's name and your note about them are sent to Anthropic to write it. That person is not a Haveo user. They did not agree to anything, and they do not know the app exists.

So a suggestion rather than a rule: write about people the way you would be comfortable with them reading. First names or initials are enough for Haveo to be useful, and it is kinder to everyone. What you write about someone is your responsibility.

Asking first

Before anything is sent to Anthropic or OpenAI, Haveo asks you and waits for you to agree. Until you do, the AI features stay switched off — and everything that runs on your phone alone keeps working as normal.

The feature counts described under "Which features get used" are not covered by that question, and they run either way. They are not asked about because they carry nothing you wrote and nothing that points to you.

You can change your mind whenever you like: Profile > AI > Let Haveo answer. Switching it off stops the chat, the file reading and the spoken voice immediately. Nothing you have already saved is deleted.

When you paste an event link

If you paste a web link while adding an event, Haveo visits that page from your phone to read the details and fill in the form. That website will see the visit the same way it would if you had opened it in your browser. Haveo does not send it anything about you.

Links that open your browser

Some parts of the app open a website: this policy, our support page, Meetup and Eventbrite when you go looking for events, a Google Form if you ask to be told when a feature is ready, and a search when you want to look up a study we mention. Those open in your own browser and are handled by whoever runs them, under their rules, not ours.

When Haveo updates itself

Small fixes can reach Haveo without you downloading a new version from the App Store. For that to work, the app asks Expo — the company whose tools Haveo is built with — whether newer app code is ready, and downloads it if there is. That request says which version of Haveo you have and what kind of phone it is, and carries a random ID for the installation. Nothing you have written is included, and the ID is not tied to a name, an email or an account, because Haveo has none of those.

Which features get used

Haveo counts which parts of the app get opened and finished. It does not record what you write in them.

There is a fixed list of about twenty things it can count, and that is the whole list. "An intro was saved." "Event Mode was opened." "A practice run finished." Each count is one name from that list. Nothing is attached to it.

Haveo can't attach your words to a count, because there is nowhere to put them. The part of the app that sends a count accepts the name and nothing else. It has no second slot, so there is no way to pass your text into it even by mistake. A test runs every time the app is built and fails if anyone adds one.

Three things travel with each count: the name, the time, and which version of Haveo you have. There is also a random number, because the service that receives the counts asks for one. That number is made when you open the app and it is not saved anywhere. Open Haveo again and it is a different number. It can't be used to recognise you, and it can't join up what you did today with what you did last week.

What a count can't include: your name, your email, your events, your notes, your reflections, the people you met, or anything you typed.

Your location is not sent either. Haveo does not ask your phone where you are, and the service is told, on every count, not to work it out from your connection.

The counts go to PostHog, a company that counts app usage for other apps too. We read them to see which parts of Haveo get used and which parts nobody opens. They are not sold, and they are not used to follow you into other apps or websites.

This runs whether or not you have turned the AI on. It is separate from the AI: it sends no words, so there is nothing about it to agree to. Apple already tells us how many people download Haveo and how many come back. Apple can't tell us which features they use, and that is the only gap these counts fill.

What Haveo does not do

  • We don't have accounts or logins
  • We don't have crash-reporting or advertising code in the app. We do count which features get opened — see "Which features get used" above — and a count can't carry anything you wrote
  • We don't use cookies or tracking pixels
  • We don't sell your data, or share it with advertisers
  • We don't access your camera, your location, your contacts list, or your photo library on our own — Haveo only opens a file picker if you tap to attach something
  • We don't have a database of your notes. The relay forwards AI requests; it does not store your conversations

Your data, your control

  • Delete an event: the event is removed from your phone right away. Notes, cheat sheet items and reflections you made for it may remain saved on your phone, and you can delete those in the places you created them.
  • Delete a contact, a note or a chat: removed from your phone immediately.
  • Delete everything: deleting the app removes everything Haveo has stored. iOS Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Haveo > Delete App.
  • Stop your words being sent: Profile > AI > Let Haveo answer, switched off. The feature counts above are separate and keep running — they carry no words, so there is nothing of yours in them to stop.

There is no "delete my account" button because there are no accounts. We cannot recover anything you delete, because we never had a copy.

Where you are

Haveo is made by a small company in Massachusetts, USA. Anthropic and OpenAI process what you send them on our behalf, under their own data protection agreements. Because your information lives on your phone rather than on our servers, most of it is only ever in your own hands. If you are in the UK or the EU and want to know what we hold about you, the honest answer is almost certainly nothing — but write to us and we will tell you.

Children's privacy

Haveo is rated 13+ on the App Store (12+ in some regions and on earlier operating systems) and is not directed at children. We don't knowingly collect any information from children under 13.
